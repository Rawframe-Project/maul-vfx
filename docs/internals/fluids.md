# Fluids

How gases, liquids and water surfaces are solved, and the choices that keep their results the same whatever the threads. The user's view is [fluids.md](../user/fluids.md).

## Gases on a staggered grid

A gas ([src/gas.h](../../src/gas.h)) is a marker-and-cell grid: velocity components live at the faces of the cells, density, temperature and fuel at their centers. Faces make the divergence of a cell exact from its own six faces, which the pressure solve needs; centers suit what is carried. Its fields live in the instance's block, double-buffered where a pass reads one and writes the other.

A gas's step ([src/gas_step.c](../../src/gas_step.c)) runs in substeps: sources and forces, then advection of the velocity and the scalars by the velocity, decay, combustion, and the pressure projection.

- **Advection** is semi-Lagrangian (Stam 1999): each point traces back along the velocity by the midpoint rule and takes the value it lands on, so the step is stable at any time step. MacCormack's correction traces the result forward again and takes back half the error, which keeps detail that plain semi-Lagrangian advection blurs; where the corrected value leaves the range of the points it was sampled from, the plain value is kept, so it cannot overshoot.
- **Forces and combustion** ([src/gas_forces.c](../../src/gas_forces.c)) follow Fedkiw, Stam and Jensen 2001 and Nguyen, Fedkiw and Jensen 2002: buoyancy from density and temperature, vorticity confinement to restore the small curls numerical dissipation removes, and fuel burning into heat, smoke and expansion.
- **Pressure** ([src/gas_pressure.c](../../src/gas_pressure.c)) makes the velocity free of divergence by a Poisson solve with geometric multigrid V cycles and weighted Jacobi smoothing (Briggs, Henson and McCormick 2000), its iteration counts fixed. Multigrid converges in a few cycles whatever the grid size, where plain Jacobi needs iterations in proportion to it. Across a closed wall the face's velocity stays zero; across an open one the pressure is zero half a cell out, so flow leaves freely.
- **Solids** ([src/gas_solid.c](../../src/gas_solid.c)) mark the cells inside obstacles; the faces beside them take the obstacles' velocities, so a moving collider pushes smoke.

Every pass is a gather: each cell or face reads the other buffer and writes only itself. That is what lets the passes spread over the host's tasks without changing a bit, and what lets the GPU part run the same passes in parallel. The one inherent scatter, particles pouring into a gas ([src/gas_splat.c](../../src/gas_splat.c)), runs serially in the particles' order, the same sums a gather visiting the particles in that order would make.

A gas lags its particles by a step: emitters step first, then gases, so a gas force reads the velocity the last step left. Sampling ([src/gas_lattice.c](../../src/gas_lattice.c)) is trilinear from a cell's or a face's array, the same code for the step and for particles that follow the gas.

## Liquids

**Position-based fluids** ([src/liquid.c](../../src/liquid.c), Macklin and Muller 2013) keep each particle at its rest density by iterations on the places, each a gather over the neighbors in the grid's order, with the smoothing kernels of Muller, Charypar and Gross 2003 ([src/liquid_kernel.h](../../src/liquid_kernel.h)). XSPH viscosity and vorticity confinement follow. Iterations are Jacobi, each particle reading the last iteration's places, so the order particles are visited in does not matter.

**APIC** ([src/gas_liquid.c](../../src/gas_liquid.c), Jiang et al. 2015) moves a liquid's particles through a liquid gas: the particles splat their velocities and their affine matrices onto the faces, the gas projects the velocity to be incompressible, and each particle takes back the projected velocity and a new affine matrix. APIC keeps the rotation that particle-in-cell loses and avoids the noise of FLIP. A fast flow splits the instance's step into substeps by the fastest particle's speed against the cell size, read from what the last step left, so the count is the same on every path.

## Surfaces of particles

A surface gas ([src/gas_surface.c](../../src/gas_surface.c)) holds the signed distance to the surface around an emitter's particles, made anew each step. [src/surface_mesh.c](../../src/surface_mesh.c) meshes it at a level by marching cubes, or marching squares in 2D. The case table ([src/marching_table.c](../../src/marching_table.c)) is generated by `tools/gen_marching_cubes.py`, not copied: it derives every case and every choice of its ambiguous faces, decides each face by the asymptotic decider from the face's four values alone, and checks before writing that random fields mesh with every edge in two triangles of opposite ways. Two cubes that share a face decide it alike, so the mesh has no cracks. The same generator writes the GPU part's copy of the table, so both paths emit the same vertices in the same order.

## Water surfaces

A water surface ([src/water.h](../../src/water.h)) is a grid of heights across X and Z of the instance's frame, as heightfield colliders lie.

- **Waves** ([src/water_step.c](../../src/water_step.c)) solve the wave equation for each cell's displacement from rest and its rate, in substeps that keep the waves' speed within the grid's stable limit.
- **Shallow water** ([src/water_shallow.c](../../src/water_shallow.c)) uses the virtual pipe model: flows through pipes between neighboring cells, driven by the difference of the water's surface heights, scaled down where they would drain a cell below zero, so the volume is kept and water can run off terrain and pool. Its ground ([src/water_ground.c](../../src/water_ground.c)) is read from a heightfield grid, which the host may change between steps.

A push the host gives sinks or lifts the water within a radius at the start of the next step, by moving volume rather than setting heights, so the displaced water rises around the hollow. Particles that meet the surface ([src/water_couple.c](../../src/water_couple.c)) take its pull (buoyancy and the water's flow) as they move, and their crossings sink it and pour into it at the next step. Every substep is a gather per cell, for the same reason as a gas's.

## Planned fluids

A planned instance's gases and water surfaces are stepped elsewhere ([gpu.md](gpu.md)). The planner resolves for each step what the CPU path would take from the instance (the box's motion, the forces, the obstacles, the pushes) and hands it over a step at a time. What the simulation alone knows comes back: whether a gas held anything and a liquid's fastest speed (`mvfxInstance_SetGasHolds`, `mvfxInstance_SetLiquidSpeed`), and a water surface's counts, which decide when a stopped instance finishes and how its next steps split.
